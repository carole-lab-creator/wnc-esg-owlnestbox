Chapter II · Xinfeng Junior High School · Spring 2026

12 Days That Stunned Everyone
A Story of Loss, Rescue, and a Second Chance

If Dadu was nature's perfect lesson, Xinfeng was its gut-punch — and then its most extraordinary surprise.

In the spring of 2026, three Collared Scops Owl chicks were born in the nest box at Xinfeng Junior High School. But nature doesn't always play it safe. What happened next would shake everyone watching — and then leave them speechless.

01
CHAPTER ONE
33% — and a Rescue Call Across the Pacific
Mar 25
One chick went missing. Another was found to have a right foot that couldn't bend — a condition present since birth. For a raptor that depends on gripping prey and climbing branches, this was a serious problem.
Apr 10
Midday
While practicing its wing flaps, the injured chick fell from the nest. It landed on the ground below — and the race to save it began immediately.
Rescue begins
School staff and teachers rushed to find a wildlife rescue contact. In a remarkable turn of events, a teacher's friend studying at Harvard University helped coordinate the rescue from across the Pacific — reaching out online to connect with Taiwan's wildlife care network.
Safe hands
The little owl was successfully transferred to the Leofoo Village Wildlife Rescue Station for medical care. Thanks to the quick response, it had a real chance — at recovery, and maybe one day, at the sky again.

33 %
The wild survival rate dropped to 33% — only one of three chicks remained free in the wild. But behind that number is a story of people who acted fast when it mattered most. The other one is still out there, waiting for the day it can fly again.
In Taiwan, Collared Scops Owls are a protected species — never keep one at home.

If you find a fallen chick in a schoolyard or on the street, here's what to do:

① Take a photo → ② Keep your distance and watch to see if a parent owl is nearby → ③ Call 1999 or contact a licensed wildlife rescue organization (like Leofoo Village Rescue Station, as in this case).

Keeping a wild animal without a permit can lead to fines or criminal charges under Taiwan's Wildlife Conservation Act. The kindest thing you can do is hand it to the experts.

CHAPTER TWO
The Mystery: A Brand New Egg, 12 Days Later

After everything that happened, something quietly wonderful appeared in the treetops above Xinfeng Junior High. On April 16, the mother owl was spotted hopping through the leaves with her one surviving chick — alive, together, carrying on.

Apr 16
The mother owl and her last surviving chick move lightly through the treetops together. Life, stubborn as ever, keeps going.
Apr 28
A researcher checked the camera feed — and had to look twice. Inside the nest box: a brand new egg. In the recorded history of Collared Scops Owls in Taiwan, this kind of thing almost never happens.

Just 12 days passed between the chick leaving the nest and a new egg appearing — which lines up perfectly with the 10–21 day biological reset cycle that raptors need before they can lay again. The leading theory: because food around the Xinfeng campus was so consistently abundant, the mother recovered her strength quickly — and may have actively chosen to attempt a "double brooding" season, maximizing her reproductive potential for the year.

Is this the same mother — or a completely new family?

Collared Scops Owls are notoriously hard to tell apart by sight. Right now, we genuinely can't be 100% sure whether this is the same female. WNC's outdoor cameras will be going live soon — and when they do, you'll be able to watch and help us figure it out.

🌳 About this initiative

This project was started — entirely from the ground up — by WNC's Mechanical and Industry Design Div., two years running. The team sponsored network cameras at 11 schools across Hsinchu and built 18 nest boxes by hand, turning technology into a force for biodiversity and citizen science.
